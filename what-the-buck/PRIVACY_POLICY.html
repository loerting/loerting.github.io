Privacy Policy for What the Buck?!

Last updated: 26 November 2026

What the Buck?! (“the game”) is developed and published by Alexander Kohl-Lörting (“I”, “me”). This policy explains what the Android version of What the Buck?! does with your data.

Short version

What the Buck?! does not collect, transmit or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. It does not need an internet connection to play.

Data stored on your device

What the Buck?! saves your game progress and your settings locally, in the app’s private storage.

If backup is turned on on your device, Android includes this data in your device backup to your Google account. I cannot read that backup. If you uninstall What the Buck?!, Android deletes the data on your device along with the app.

Data sent off your device

None. What the Buck?! makes no network requests of its own and contains no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or attribution SDKs.

Links that open outside the game

What the Buck?! has buttons that open external pages in your browser or in another app. Opening one is always your choice, and once you leave the game you are covered by that service’s own privacy policy, not this one. Those destinations are:

  • Steam: store.steampowered.com
  • Discord: discord.gg
  • Instagram: www.instagram.com
  • the other social media pages of Loerting Games, such as YouTube, TikTok, X and Bluesky
  • the press kit, a folder on Google Drive: drive.google.com
  • a feedback form made with Google Forms, if you choose to send feedback. Your answers are stored in my Google account and used only to read and act on your feedback (legal basis: my legitimate interest in improving the game, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); I delete them when they are no longer needed for that. Google’s privacy policy covers Google’s own processing.

Purchases

On Google Play, What the Buck?! is free to download. One in-app purchase unlocks the full version; there are no other purchases in the game. Google Play processes the payment. The game only receives Google Play’s confirmation that the full version was bought, and I never see your payment details. Google’s handling of that transaction is covered by the Google Play Terms of Service and Google’s privacy policy.

For each purchase, Google Play gives the developer account a sales record with the order number, the price, the device model and the buyer’s country, region, postcode and, in some countries, city. This record is used only for accounting and is kept for as long as Austrian tax law requires, which is seven years (legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).

On Google Play, my brother Leonhard distributes the game on my behalf through his developer account. The sales records described above go to his developer account. He keeps them himself, as Austrian tax law requires, and passes on only monthly totals to me. For these records he is the controller; you can reach him at his address in the imprint.

Children

What the Buck?! contains no data collection, no ads and no social features, so there is nothing for it to collect from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted at this URL and the date at the top will change.

How the data is protected

Your game progress stays on your device and, if backup is turned on, in your Android backup. The sales records are kept in the Google Play developer account and, when downloaded, only on the account holder’s own computer. The feedback answers are kept in my Google account. Only the account holder can sign in to these accounts. The Google Play developer account is protected with 2-Step Verification.

Your rights

You have the right to access, correct, delete and restrict the processing of your personal data and to data portability (Art. 15 to 20 GDPR). To use them, write to the contact address below. You can also lodge a complaint with the Austrian data protection authority, the Datenschutzbehörde.

Right to object

Where I process your data on the basis of my legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), that is, your answers in the feedback form, you can object to this processing at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21(1) GDPR). Write to the contact address below. I will then stop processing your answers unless I can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or I need them to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.
